Subject: DBMS
Unit 2: Relational Model and Normalization
Lecture Notes by Prof. R. Sharma

Chapter Overview:
This unit covers the foundational theoretical principles of relational databases, relational algebra, and database normalization techniques to eliminate data anomalies.

Key Concepts:
- Relational Schema: Attributes, Tuples, Candidate Keys, Primary Keys, Foreign Keys
- Integrity Constraints: Entity integrity, Referential integrity, Domain constraints
- Functional Dependencies: Full functional dependency, Partial dependency, Transitive dependency
- Normal Forms:
  - First Normal Form (1NF): Eliminates repeating groups and ensures attribute values are atomic.
  - Second Normal Form (2NF): In 1NF and all non-prime attributes are fully functionally dependent on the primary key.
  - Third Normal Form (3NF): In 2NF and no non-prime attribute is transitively dependent on candidate keys.
  - Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF): For every functional dependency X -> Y, X must be a superkey.
- Relational Algebra Operations: Selection, Projection, Cartesian Product, Natural Join, Theta Join

Important Definitions:
Candidate key: A minimal superkey that uniquely identifies tuples in a relation.
Prime attribute: An attribute that is a member of any candidate key of the relation.
Lossless Join Decomposition: Ensures that decomposing relation R into R1 and R2 preserves the original information without spurious tuples.
